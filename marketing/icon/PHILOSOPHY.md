# Condensation

**A visual philosophy of the moment something passing becomes something kept.**

Condensation sees every form as a state change. A single continuous gesture, a line or a spiral or a path, starts out scattered at its edge: loose particles that barely hold together. As it moves inward it gathers into a solid, unbroken mark. The work never shows a before and an after side by side. It shows the transition itself, drawn as one motion, so the eye watches vapour turn into matter without a seam. Every interval between marks is computed, never guessed. The composition should read as the product of painstaking calibration, where one wrong gap would break the illusion.

Space comes in two densities. The outer field is generous, dark and quiet, and the few marks in it seem to float. The centre is dense, dark and heavy, and it has weight. Nothing is framed off as separate. Density alone draws the boundary between air and object. Form is strictly geometric: perfect circles and true spirals, with radii that step in exact increments. The result should feel machined, like the output of a master craftsman's lathe rather than a hand sketch.

Colour is kept to a minimum and used as heat. The field is a deep, near-black violet with warmth bleeding in from one edge. The marks are pale and cool while they are scattered and turn fully opaque as they settle. A single molten gradient, crimson moving to ember orange, sits only at the point of full condensation, the one place where the work allows itself to glow. Every hue is chosen to feel inevitable.

Scale works in two registers at once. From across a room the piece reduces to one bold emblem: a dark disc with a burning core. Up close it reveals a dense accumulation of marks, a rhythm of dots becoming dashes becoming line, that rewards sustained looking. That dual legibility is the mark of master-level execution. The same image has to work at a glance and under a loupe.

Text is absent. Structure and light carry the meaning. Sheen and shadow are used sparingly, only to confirm that the condensed centre is a physical object you could hold. Everything is refined again and again until nothing can be taken away.
